Dimitrios S. Nikolopoulos — Short Bio

Dimitrios S. Nikolopoulos is the John W. Hancock Professor of Engineering and
Professor of Computer Science at Virginia Tech, where he also holds a courtesy
appointment in the Bradley Department of Electrical and Computer Engineering.
He directs the Performance Engineering for Emerging Architectures Laboratory
(PEARL) and serves as Associate Director of the Stack@CS Center for Computer
Systems. He is additionally an Honorary Professor at Queen's University Belfast
and an Adjunct Professor at Old Dominion University.

His research addresses the programming and orchestration of the multiprocessors
and accelerators underpinning high-performance computing, cloud computing, and
artificial intelligence. His current interests are efficient and resilient AI
systems across the computing continuum, performance engineering for modern
accelerators, and systems for data-intensive science and cyberinfrastructure,
with an overarching goal of establishing sustainable AI practices by reducing
and rationalizing the infrastructure footprint of AI. His contributions have
shaped the OpenMP parallel programming standard and have been adopted in Red Hat
Enterprise Linux and in commercial compilers, in-memory databases, and data
stream processing systems.

He is a Fellow of the IEEE, the Royal Society (Wolfson), the Asia-Pacific
Artificial Intelligence Association, the British Computer Society, the
Institution of Engineering and Technology, the International AI Industry
Alliance, and HiPEAC; a Distinguished Member of the ACM; and a Distinguished
Visitor of the IEEE Computer Society. His awards include the NSF CAREER, DOE
Early Career, IBM, Cisco, and Sony faculty awards, the SFI-DEL Investigator
Award, and a Marie Curie Individual Fellowship, along with eight best-paper
awards at venues including SC, PPoPP, IPDPS, CCGRID, and DATE.

He chairs the steering committee of the ACM International Conference on
Supercomputing (ICS) and has served as Program Chair of HPDC (2026), ICS (2022,
2023), and CCGRID (2014), and as General Chair of IEEE Cluster (2010, 2018) and
IEEE ISPASS (2018). He previously served as Head of the School of Electronics,
Electrical Engineering and Computer Science and Director of the ECIT Institute
at Queen's University Belfast. He has mentored 36 Ph.D. students and 17
postdoctoral researchers, and has secured research funding totalling $140.8
million across 54 awards.

He received his Diploma of Engineering (1996), M.Eng. (1997), and Ph.D. (2000)
in Computer Engineering and Informatics from the University of Patras, Greece.
